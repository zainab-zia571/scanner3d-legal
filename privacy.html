Privacy Policy

Last updated 8 September 2026

Introduction

This policy explains what the 3D Scanner Android application (com.scanner3d.app) does with information. The short version is that scanning, reconstruction and storage all happen on your device, and nothing is sent anywhere unless you ask for it.

Cloud processing is optional and off until you turn it on. There is no account and nothing to sign in to. If you never enable it, nothing you scan leaves your phone except as a file you deliberately share, and the sections below about uploading do not describe anything that has happened to you.

An earlier version of this application declared no INTERNET permission at all, and this policy said so. That is no longer true, and it is stated plainly here rather than quietly dropped: the application now declares it, because cloud processing needs it. What has not changed is that the default is local, the choice is yours, and the application asks before the first upload.

By using the application you agree to this policy. If you do not agree, please do not use the application.

Information We Collect

With cloud processing off, we collect nothing. No personal information, no usage statistics, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers, no contact details. Nothing is transmitted, so there is nothing to receive or store.

With cloud processing on, we receive the scan you chose to send and nothing else about you. That means the photographs, depth images and camera positions of that scan, and an anonymous identifier described below. We do not receive your name, your email address, your location, your contacts or any identifier issued by Android or by Google.

The application also creates and holds data on your device, described below. That data is yours.

Scan Data

Scanning is the purpose of the application, and it produces the most sensitive data it handles. A scan can include:

  • Depth measurements and three-dimensional point data.
  • Photographs taken during capture, used to colour the finished model. These are ordinary camera images and may show whatever was in view, including people, documents or the inside of your home.
  • Camera positions and orientations relative to where the scan began.
  • Reconstructed models, textures and any measurements you take.

All of it is written to the application's private storage on the device. Treat a scan as you would a photograph of the same place.

No location data. The application does not request location permission and does not record where a scan was taken. Positions inside a scan are relative to its own starting point and say nothing about where on Earth you were.

Cloud Processing

Reconstruction is demanding, and a phone is slower at it than a rented graphics card. You may choose, in Settings → Processing, to have a scan reconstructed on a remote machine instead. The application asks you to agree the first time, and shows you what this section says before it sends anything.

What is uploaded. For the scan you chose, and only that scan:

  • its depth images, as measured, and the camera position for each;
  • its photographs, resized to a limit that depends on the quality you pick;
  • a short description of the scan — which kind it is, how it was captured, and whether it has a known real-world scale;
  • an anonymous install identifier: a random value the application generates on your device the first time you enable cloud processing. It exists to keep one installation from consuming another's allowance. It is not derived from your phone, your account or anything about you, it is not shared with anyone else, and turning cloud processing off deletes it.
  • a subscription identifier, if you subscribe: a separate value that identifies your purchase to RevenueCat, so that the service can confirm you are entitled to what you are asking for. It is kept apart from the install identifier above and outlives it, deliberately — if turning cloud processing off deleted it, it would also lose track of a subscription you are still paying for. It is not your name, your email address or your Google account, and we never receive those.

Photographs of a room contain whatever was in the room. Consider that before sending a scan, exactly as you would before sharing one.

What is not uploaded. Scans you did not choose to send. Measurements, project names, or anything else held on the device. Any identifier that could link the job to you personally.

How long it is kept. Uploads and the results made from them are deleted within seven days, automatically, by a retention rule on the storage itself rather than by anyone remembering to run it. They are not used to train anything, are not analysed, and are not shared. Your own copy on your phone is unaffected and stays until you delete it.

Where it goes. To storage and compute rented from the providers named under Third Party Services, over an encrypted connection. Reconstruction runs there and the result comes back to your phone.

Turning it off. Setting processing back to On this device stops any further upload immediately and deletes the install identifier. Anything already uploaded is deleted by the retention rule; if you would like it removed sooner, ask us.

Device Information

The application checks what your device is capable of so that it can offer the scanning modes that will actually work: whether ARCore is available, whether a depth sensor is present, how much memory and free storage there is, and the camera's characteristics. This check happens on the device, is used only to decide what to show you, and is not transmitted.

Permissions

These are the permissions the installed application declares, in full. The list is taken from the built application file rather than from memory, and it is checked against it before each release.

  • Camera — the only permission you are asked to grant. It is required to see what you are scanning. Android will prompt for it, and you may withdraw it at any time in system settings, after which scanning will not function.
  • Internet (INTERNET) — required to upload a scan for cloud processing and to fetch the result. It is used for nothing else: no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no checking in. With cloud processing off, the application opens no connection at all.
  • View network connections (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) — used to check that a usable network exists before starting an upload, so a large transfer is not begun on a connection that cannot finish it, and to tell you when you are about to send over mobile data. It reveals whether you are connected, not what you do while connected.
  • In-app billing (com.android.vending.BILLING) — lets the application ask Google Play to sell a subscription and to say whether you hold one. It is Google Play that takes the payment and Google Play that holds the card; this application never sees a card number, and declaring this permission gives it no access to your Google account.
  • Foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE) and foreground service, data sync (FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC) — building a three-dimensional model from a scan takes minutes of continuous work. These allow that work to carry on while the application is not on screen, instead of Android stopping it and losing what had been done. Nothing is synchronised with anywhere: “data sync” is simply the category Android provides for work that has to finish, and there is no category named “finish building a model”.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — to show you what is being built while it happens, which Android requires of any application doing work in the background. You may refuse it; the work still runs, and you simply do not see the notice. It is never used to advertise anything to you.
  • An internal receiver permission (DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION) — generated automatically by the Android support libraries so that the application's own components cannot be reached by other applications. It grants no access to anything on your device.

These are not declared, and so cannot be used:

  • Location, contacts, microphone, phone, or external storage. Scans are kept in the application's own private directory. A file reaches your gallery or file manager only when you export or share it yourself.
  • Accounts, or any device identifier. The application cannot read who you are signed in as, and does not ask Android for an advertising identifier or a device serial.

Data Storage

Scans, photographs, models and measurements are stored in the application's private folder on your device, which the Android system keeps separate from other applications. They remain there until you delete them.

Deleting a scan in the application removes its files and its database entry from the device. Uninstalling the application removes everything it created.

If a scan was sent for cloud processing, a copy exists off your device until the seven-day retention rule removes it. Deleting the scan on your phone does not shorten that, and the rule runs whether or not you delete anything. To have a copy removed sooner, ask us — you will need to say which scan, which the application can tell you.

Export and Sharing

The application can write a model to a file in a number of standard formats, and hand that file to another application you choose — a messaging app, a cloud drive, an email client. This and cloud processing are the only ways data leaves the application, and both happen only when you ask.

Once you have shared a file, it is governed by whatever terms apply to the application or service you shared it with, not by this policy. Consider what a scan shows before sending it.

The application provides no cloud backup and no synchronisation. Cloud processing is not storage: what is uploaded is deleted within seven days, and nothing there is a copy you can come back for. If you want a scan kept elsewhere, exporting it is how you make one.

Third Party Services

The application uses Google Play Services for AR (ARCore), a Google component installed on your device, for motion tracking and depth estimation. ARCore runs on the device as part of the Android system. Its behaviour is governed by Google's terms and privacy policy rather than by this one:

  • policies.google.com/privacy
  • developers.google.com/ar/data-privacy

If you use cloud processing, these providers handle your scan on our behalf. They process it to perform the reconstruction and for nothing else.

  • Cloudflare — stores the uploaded scan and the result, and runs the service that hands work out. The seven-day deletion is a rule on that storage. cloudflare.com/privacypolicy
  • RunPod — provides the graphics hardware the reconstruction runs on. runpod.io/legal/privacy-policy
  • RevenueCat — manages the subscription and tells us whether one is active. It is given an app user identifier, which the application generates and which is not your name, your email address or your Google account, together with the purchase the store reports. It is what the service asks before it accepts a cloud job, so that the answer does not depend on your phone, which could be made to say anything. revenuecat.com/privacy
  • Google Play Billing — takes the payment. Google never passes us your card details, and we never ask Google who you are. policies.google.com/privacy

Beyond these and ARCore, the application integrates no third-party service: no analytics, no advertising network, no crash reporting, no social sign-in.

Security

Data is held in application-private storage, which Android protects from other applications. With cloud processing off, the strongest protection is structural rather than technical: data that is never transmitted cannot be intercepted, and data held on no server cannot be exposed by a breach of one.

With cloud processing on, that structural protection is partly given up, and saying otherwise would be dishonest. What replaces it: the upload travels over an encrypted connection; it is held in private storage that is not listable or guessable and is reachable only through links that expire; and it is deleted within seven days. The keys to that storage are held by the service, never inside the application, so a copy of the app cannot be taken apart to reach anybody's data.

What remains is the security of the device itself. A screen lock and full device encryption — both standard on modern Android — are what protect your scans if the phone is lost. No method of electronic storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

User Rights

Rights of access, correction, deletion and portability normally require a request to whoever holds your data. Here you hold it, so you exercise them directly:

  • Access — open any scan in the application.
  • Portability — export a scan in a standard format.
  • Deletion — delete a scan, or uninstall the application.
  • Withdrawing consent — revoke the camera permission in Android settings, and set processing back to On this device to stop any further upload and delete the install identifier.
  • Erasure of an upload — uploads are deleted within seven days regardless. To have one removed sooner, ask us.

If you would still like to make a request or ask a question about this policy, please contact us.

Children's Privacy

The application is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, child or adult. Parents should be aware that scanning captures camera images of whatever is in view; that those images stay on the device unless cloud processing is deliberately turned on; and that cloud processing requires a paid subscription, which a child should not be arranging unsupervised. We would encourage supervision on that basis.
